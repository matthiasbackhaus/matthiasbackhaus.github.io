Expected Intraday Range and the VIX: Calibration and a Touch-Probability Backtest on Nasdaq-100 Regular-Trading-Hours Sessions

We study whether the practitioner rule VIX/√252 is a well-calibrated estimate of the intraday range of the Nasdaq-100 during U.S. regular trading hours, and how often the implied band is touched. Using one-minute data on 2,909 RTH sessions (January 2015 – April 2026) and prior-day VIX closes, we find the calibration slope is 1.001 — the naive rule is nearly unbiased on average. The ±VIX/√252 band is touched in 37.5% of sessions, with touch probability rising monotonically from 31.7% (VIX < 14) to 43.9% (VIX > 25), identifying a state-dependent bias that mirrors the volatility risk premium. Median first-passage time is 144 minutes; the lower band is reached first in 54.8% of hit sessions.

July 2026 · Working Paper

A Cross-Asset Demand-Zone Strategy for Nasdaq-100 Futures: Design, Backtest, and Path-Risk Analysis at a Fixed 1:2.2 Payoff

A fully mechanical intraday strategy for Nasdaq-100 futures (NQ/MNQ) built on demand zones derived from the QQQ ETF as a reference instrument. Demand zones are identified from the prior session of QQQ using a base-plus-explosive-candle pattern; entries are confirmed by an above-average-volume bar in the first 90 minutes of the cash session; every position uses a fixed 1:2.2 reward-to-risk ratio. Tested on one-minute data from January 2022 to April 2026 (463 trades, three micro contracts): profit factor 1.36, recovery factor 5.38, total profit $18,198, maximum drawdown $3,384. A 10,000-path Monte Carlo characterises path risk, streak distributions, and monthly return decomposition.
